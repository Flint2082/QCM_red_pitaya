MODE
Measurement Mode
Compensated locks both modes and solves the crystal temperature from the temp mode, so the thickness is corrected for it. Uncompensated locks the mass mode only — GET LOCK parks the temp oscillator at 0 Hz — and reports the plain thickness from the mass-mode shift, with no temperature: every frequency change is taken as film, so it is only as good as the crystal's temperature is steady. For a crystal with no usable temp mode or no calibration. GET LOCK uses the new mode straight away. Switched to Uncompensated, the thickness is the uncompensated one from the current reference at once; back to Compensated, GET LOCK (to lock the temp mode) and ZERO for a compensated reference. Either mode is also parked by setting its lock frequency to 0 Hz

SAMPLING & RATE
Sample Rate
How often the frequencies are read and the thickness and deposition rate worked out. The FPGA updates its frequency registers far faster, so this is the software's pace only. A higher rate puts more samples into each rate window, which is what keeps a short, quick-reacting window usable; for a given window it hardly changes the noise, which comes from slow frequency wander. Lock detection and the drive-gain servo keep the same timing at any rate. 100 Hz takes about half of one CPU core; that load warms the board and shifts its reference clock by a few tens of ppb, which reads as thickness, so change the rate a few minutes before a run rather than just before. A change waits for a run in progress to end. The rate actually achieved shows at the bottom of the page, and a run that falls behind says so in its log. 1–100 Hz
Hz
Rate Window
The deposition rate is the slope of a straight line fitted through this much of the latest thickness, every sample of it, for both the compensated and the uncompensated rate. A longer window gives a quieter rate that follows a change more slowly, lagging it by about half the window. It needs two samples in half a window, so at 10 Hz keep it at 0.2 s or more. The rate stays blank until half a window of the run has been taken. Takes effect immediately, a run in progress included, where a longer window blanks the rate again until half of it has been taken; the run log records the window in its SETTINGS row. 0.1–300 s
s
Display / PLC Rate
How often a sample is sent to this page, and every other open browser, and written to the PLC. Each one is the newest sample, its rate fitted to every sample before it. Old lab PCs draw 10 Hz comfortably. Takes effect immediately. 1–25 Hz
Hz
Log Rate
How often the run log takes a sample; 0 logs every sample. Each row carries the deposition rate fitted to every sample, whatever this is set to. A row is about 300 bytes: about 250 MB a day at 10 Hz, 2.6 GB if every sample at 100 Hz is logged, so mind the free space on the SD card (System → Storage) before a long run. Takes effect immediately; a change during a run is noted in its log. 0–100 Hz

Drive Gain Control
Automatic scales the excitation sinewave (FPGA register output_gain_1) to hold the demodulated amplitude at the target below, keeping the loop's signal-to-noise — and the phase noise the lock detection judges on — steady as the crystal's response falls off with temperature and film loading. Only ever adjusted while this mode is locked: off resonance the returned amplitude says nothing about the drive a resonance needs, so an unlocked channel falls back to the manual gain — as do sweeps and the capacitor adjustment, which want a fixed known drive. Manual is the override: the manual gain applies at all times
Manual Drive Gain
Amplitude multiplier on the output sinewave whenever the AGC is not adjusting: always in Manual, and on either setting while this mode is unlocked. Runs 0 to 0.5, the register being all fraction and capped at half scale: 1.0 would be the undivided drive the hardware put out before the gain register existed, so even the maximum here is half of that and the default of 0.25 is a quarter — amplitudes read on an older build do not carry over. With the AGC on, the default leaves it a factor of two of drive above it before it rails
×

LOCK DETECTION
Phase Noise Limit
Maximum phase-error standard deviation ("lock quality", shown live on the Monitor tab) to count as locked. Replaces the old amplitude threshold — phase noise rises when the signal weakens, without needing a level that changes with temperature and film loading. Watch the live value on a good lock, then set this comfortably above it
rad
Phase Tolerance
Maximum deviation of the mean phase error from the lock point (∓π/2 depending on feedback inversion) to count as locked
rad
LOCK RECOVERY
Auto Re-lock
Re-acquire automatically if the mass mode loses lock during a measurement — that is the mode the thickness is measured from, and nothing in software can retrieve it off resonance. A temp-mode-only loss never triggers one: re-locking resets both oscillators and interrupts the thickness output for a few seconds (the raw readings keep streaming), which would drag the mass loop through a re-acquisition it did not need, and on marginal lock detection it repeats that indefinitely while breaking the loops it is trying to rescue. The temp loop is left running with its capture window where the lock was last good and usually returns on its own; GET LOCK re-acquires both on demand
Hold Temperature On Lock Loss
Hold the last calculated temperature while the temp mode is unlocked, rather than solving it from an off-resonance Ft. Only the thermal correction pauses: the mass mode is still measured and the compensated thickness keeps coming out, following the film sample for sample. Held samples show as HELD on the Monitor tab.

Disabled, the temperature is solved from whatever Ft currently reads, locked or not — the live figure, with the lock flag and lock quality beside it to say how far to trust it. Useful when lock detection is marginal and the loops are in fact fine. Either way a sample is produced every cycle and the run is never interrupted
Track Lock Frequencies
While both modes are locked, keep the start frequencies on the frequencies the PLLs are actually holding, so a re-lock aims at where the resonances are now instead of where they were hours ago. Does not change the crystal profile on disk

FEEDBACK
Feedback Source
The temperature the loop regulates. PT100 sits on the holder and reads once a second. Crystal is the crystal's own temperature solved from its two locked modes with the active calibration: no lag behind the crystal, but it needs both modes locked and a profile calibrated on this crystal, so it is no use while calibrating a new one. PT100 + crystal combines the two (see below): the PT100 decides where the temperature sits, the crystal steadies it from second to second. Without a fault-free reading the drive drops to zero
PT100 + CRYSTAL
How It Works
The loop regulates on the crystal's temperature plus an offset: PT100 minus crystal, averaged over the Averaging Time. Over longer times than that the PT100 decides, so it ends up at the setpoint and stays the reference for every calibration point. Over shorter ones the loop follows the crystal, which reads ten times a second with next to no noise, runs ahead of the PT100, and feels what the PT100 never sees, such as heat lost to the air. Only the crystal's changes count, never its absolute reading, so the calibration being made does not decide the temperature. The crystal is dropped, and the loop runs on the PT100 alone with the PID gains above, while either mode is unlocked, when its reading jumps by more than 0.5 °C at once (a mode hop) or when it is more than 5 °C from the PT100. It is used again after 30 s of steady readings (three PT100 Delays, if longer), over which the offset is worked out afresh

The integral only moves within this distance of the setpoint, with either set of gains, or where the temperature has stalled short of it. AUTOTUNE
Relay Amplitude
The autotune drives ± this much around the drive needed to hold the setpoint, switching each time the temperature crosses it, so the loop oscillates at its natural period. It measures the period and the swing, works out the gains, takes them and goes on holding the setpoint. Run it at a setpoint in the middle of the range you use, with the temperature already close to it; it takes four or more oscillations, often 20–60 min

How the measured loop becomes gains. Tyreus-Luyben is gentle and robust, the usual choice for a thermal loop. No overshoot is slower still on the proportional gain. Ziegler-Nichols is fast and overshoots by about a quarter

GENERATE
Temperature Range
Lowest and highest calibration point. Range matters more than the number of points, and the temperatures you deposit at should sit in the middle of it: points below room temperature are worth the most. Keep the lowest above the room's dew point, or water condenses on the crystal
to°C
Step
Spacing of the points. A cubic has four coefficients, so take at least 6–8 distinct temperatures
°C
Pattern
Interleaved: every other temperature on the way up, the ones in between on the way down, so neighbouring points are approached from opposite sides and a drift or a heating/cooling difference shows up instead of tilting the curve. Both ways takes every temperature going up and again coming down: twice the points, and a direct hysteresis check. Up only is a plain staircase, the least robust
Reference Temperature
Where the reference points are taken, and where the TEC goes at the end when At The End says so
°C
Break-in & Anneal
One soak before the points, at this temperature, for at least this long and until the mass mode drifts less than this. A freshly mounted crystal sheds mass at ~0.5 Hz/min, falling ~30× over three hours at room temperature, and points taken through that bake the drift into the curve; heat speeds that up. Holding a little above the highest point also makes an irreversible shift, like the −3.8 Hz mass-mode step at 60 °C on 2 Nov, happen before the points instead of among them. The drift is judged at this temperature, where the loss runs fastest. For a fresh crystal give Step Timeout room: it counts from the end of the minimum. 0 min and 0 Hz/min = none
°C,min,Hz/min
Reference Points
A point at the reference straight after the break-in & anneal, and one at the very end. Comparing them shows a drift or a step over the calibration
